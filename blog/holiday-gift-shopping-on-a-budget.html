Holiday Gift Shopping on a Budget: A List, a Limit, and a Pause

4 min read

Gift shopping is the easiest spending to excuse, because it's for someone else. That's also why it gets away from people. One more thing for a niece, a nicer version for a parent, something small for yourself while you're there. Each one is kind, and the total still arrives in January.

Here's a plan that keeps the giving and loses the surprise.

1. Start With People, Not Products

Write down everyone you're buying for. Only names at first. A list of people is short and has an end. A list of things you could buy doesn't.

2. Put a Number Next to Each Name

Decide what you'll spend on each person before you look at a single store. Add the numbers up. If the total is more than you can spend, change it now: lower a few numbers, or agree with family to draw names. It's much easier to adjust a list than a credit card bill.

3. Keep Ideas on a Want List, Not in a Cart

When you find something that might suit someone, write it next to their name with the price. Don't add it to a cart. A cart is built to be finished, and stores will remind you about it until you do. Your own list lets you compare three ideas for one person and pick one.

4. Leave Yourself Off Until January

The classic holiday overspend is one for them, one for me. If something for yourself catches your eye, write it on a separate list with a date in January. If you still want it then, you can decide with a clear head and a known balance.

5. Watch the Add-Ons at Checkout

Gift wrap, faster shipping, a protection plan, something small to reach free shipping: these show up on the last page, when you already feel finished. Check the order total against the number next to that person's name. If it's over, something comes out.

6. Put a Pause at Every Checkout

Not Yet: Checkout Blocker is a free Chrome extension that covers checkout pages with a pause screen before you pay. It gives you a moment to check the order against your list.

  • Every checkout gets a pause. You retype a freshly generated code to continue, so each order is a decision.
  • Swap the code for questions if you'd rather think it through, like "Do I already own something like this?"
  • Pause a store for the day. Block a site completely for 1 to 24 hours once you've bought what you came for. It lifts itself afterward.
  • Limit the browsing. Put a time limit on the stores you wander in, anywhere from 1 to 240 minutes a visit.

7. Check the List Twice

Once a week, mark who's done and add up what you've spent. When a name is crossed off, stop shopping for that person, even if you see something better. Done is the goal.
