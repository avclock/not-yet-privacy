How to Stop Late-Night Online Shopping

4 min read

If you look at the orders you regret, a lot of them were probably placed late at night. It makes sense. The day is over, you're tired, the house is quiet, and the store is right there on a screen you were already holding. A purchase feels like a small reward for getting through the day.

The good news is that this habit depends on convenience more than most. Take away a little of the convenience and it loses a lot of its pull.

Why Night Is Different

Two things work against you after about 10pm. You're tired, and tired people find it harder to say no. And nothing interrupts you: no meeting, no errand, nobody asking what you're looking at. An hour of browsing passes without a single natural stopping point, and stores are designed so the next thing is always one tap away.

None of this is a character flaw. It does mean that deciding to "just have more willpower at night" is the plan least likely to work. Change the setup instead.

Seven Things That Help

  1. Make the morning the decision time. One rule: nothing gets bought after 9pm. Anything you find goes in the cart or a wish list. In the morning, with coffee, most of it looks different.
  2. Remove your saved cards. Take them out of the stores you use most and out of your browser's autofill. Getting out of bed to find your wallet is often enough.
  3. Log out of store apps, or delete the ones you open out of habit. You can still shop in the browser when you mean to.
  4. Charge your phone outside the bedroom. It's the oldest advice on this list and still the most effective. A $10 alarm clock removes the excuse.
  5. Turn off sale notifications. A "flash sale ends at midnight" alert at 10:40pm is aimed at exactly this moment.
  6. Give the urge somewhere else to go. You're usually after a way to wind down, and the purchase is incidental. Keep something easy within reach: a book, a show you've saved, a stretch, a note to a friend.
  7. Put a pause at checkout. For the nights the other six don't hold.

A Pause for the Nights That Get Past You

Not Yet: Checkout Blocker is a free Chrome extension we built for that last step. When you reach a checkout or payment page, it covers the page with a pause screen. To continue, you retype a freshly generated code, character by character. It can't be memorized or waited out, and at midnight, typing it is often enough to make you notice what you're doing.

Two free settings are useful at night in particular:

  • Pause a site for a while. Block a store completely for 1 to 24 hours. Set it when you sit down for the evening and it lifts by itself.
  • Time limits. Give a site anywhere from 1 to 240 minutes. A small countdown sits in the corner, and when it runs out you land on your Quiet Place, a calm page with a few things to do instead.

There's no account, and nothing you do is sent anywhere. Not Yet works in Chrome on a computer, so pair it with the phone steps above.

Common Questions

Why do I shop online late at night?

By the end of the day you're tired, and tired people find it harder to turn things down. Shopping is also an easy way to wind down: it's quiet, it's on your phone, and it gives a small lift. Stores are open, and nothing interrupts you.

How do I stop buying things on my phone at night?

Make night-time buying harder than night-time browsing. Remove saved cards, log out of store apps, charge your phone outside the bedroom, and leave anything you find in the cart until morning.

Is there an app that blocks online shopping at night?

On Chrome, Not Yet: Checkout Blocker pauses every checkout page until you retype a fresh code, and can block a shopping site completely for 1 to 24 hours or put a time limit on it.
