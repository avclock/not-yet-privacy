Black Friday 2026 Without the Regret: A Calmer Plan for the Sales

4 min read

Black Friday is Friday, November 27, 2026, the day after Thanksgiving in the US, and Cyber Monday is November 30. Plenty of stores start their "Black Friday" deals well before that, so the pressure tends to build all month.

A sale can be a good time to buy what you were going to buy anyway. The trouble is everything else. Here's a plan for getting the first part without the second.

1. Make Your List Before the Ads Start

Write down what you actually need or planned to give: gifts, something worn out, something you've wanted for months. Next to each item, write the most you'd pay. A deal on something that isn't on the list isn't a saving. It's a new purchase.

2. Pick One Number for the Whole Weekend

Decide on a total before the sales start, and write it next to the list. When that total is spent, the sales are over for you, whatever the banners say.

3. Know What the Page Is Doing

Countdown timers, "only 3 left," "12 people are looking at this," free shipping if you add one more thing: each of these is built to speed you up. None of them is a reason to buy. When you notice one, take it as your cue to slow down.

4. Sleep On Anything That Isn't on the List

If something tempting shows up that you didn't plan for, write it on a separate list and look at it the next morning. If you still want it then, and it fits your number, go ahead. Most of the time, you won't.

5. Put a Pause at Every Checkout

Not Yet: Checkout Blocker is a free Chrome extension that covers checkout pages with a pause screen before you pay. It's made for exactly this kind of weekend.

  • Every checkout gets a pause. You retype a freshly generated code to continue, so buying takes a decision, not a reflex.
  • Swap the code for questions if you'd rather think it through. The built-in ones include "Do I already own something like this?" and "Will I still want this in 24 hours? In a week?"
  • Pause a store for the day. Block a site completely for 1 to 24 hours, and it lifts itself afterward.
  • Limit the browsing. Put a time limit on deal sites, anywhere from 1 to 240 minutes a visit.

6. Count What You Kept

When you walk away from a checkout, Not Yet can add the total to a running "saved" number. By Cyber Monday night, that number is a nice thing to look at.

After the Weekend

Check your orders against your list while the return windows are still open. If something arrived that you wouldn't buy again, sending it back is part of the plan, not a failure of it.
